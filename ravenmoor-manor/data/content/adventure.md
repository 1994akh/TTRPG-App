# Death at Ravenmoor Manor

## At a glance

On All Hallows' Eve, Lord Silas Ravenmoor dies in a locked study minutes after the whole household sees the family ghost glide across the gallery. The party has until the midnight bell to prove the ghost was a man in a mask, and to unmask him.

|  |  |
|---|---|
| Party | 5–6 characters, level 5, mixed experience |
| Length | One 3-hour session |
| Combat | One climactic fight in the chapel crypt |
| Tone | Gothic Halloween with a Saturday-morning-cartoon twist: spooky, a little silly, always solvable |
| Rules | D&D 2024 rules (the newest edition), plus six house rules |

**The hook.** Each character receives Silas's invitation (Handout 1): 200 gp apiece to attend the Ravenmoor Hallows' Masque as guests and quiet bodyguards, because "someone under my roof wishes me dead before midnight." It is a costume party, so ask each player to describe their character's costume.

**Bring to the table:** 12 tealights or paper candle cards, a bag of wrapped candy (2 per player plus extras), the printed handouts, clue cards and DM screen.

**The rest of the kit (in this module):** the *DM Guide* journal for prep, rulings and what-ifs; the actors for stat blocks and roleplay profiles; the *Handouts & Clues* journal for every printable card, revealed to players one at a time; the *Locations* journal, linked from every map pin.

## The truth (DM eyes only)

Dr. Thaddeus Quill, the kindly family physician, poisoned Silas, dressed as the Lantern Widow to blame the ghost, and stole the new will. If it stays lost, the old will hands the manor to Edric, whose debts Quill secretly owns.

**The legend.** Thirty years ago Silas's first wife, Elspeth, drowned in the manor pond on All Hallows' Eve. Villagers say she returns every Hallows' Eve as the Lantern Widow, a veiled woman with a green lantern who claims one Ravenmoor life.

**The motive.** While treating Silas, Quill learned of a vein of moonsilver beneath the chapel crypt. He quietly bought up all of Edric's gambling debts. Under the old will Edric inherits the manor, and Quill would seize it to settle those debts. Silas's new will gives the manor to the village of Hollow Brook as an orphanage paid for by the moonsilver, with small stipends for family and staff. If the new will is read at midnight, Quill gets nothing.

**The method.**

1. **Poison.** At the feast, Quill gave Silas his nightly heart tonic laced with Banshee's Breath, an alchemical poison. It acts in about 45 minutes, chills the skin and freezes the face in terror. It looks exactly like death by fright, and it smells faintly of mint.
2. **The locked room.** Silas locked himself in his study at 8:25, as he always does. A forgotten passage runs from behind the study bookcase down to the chapel crypt. Quill came up it, found Silas dead, took the new will from its lockbox, splashed pond water from a flask and opened the window.
3. **The ghost.** Back in the crypt, Quill put on a pale gown, a veil and gloves smeared with glowing foxfire fungus paste, and lit a green-glass lantern. He climbed the servants' stair to the minstrels' gallery, appeared above the Great Hall and blew out every candle with a pinch of alchemical snuffing powder.
4. **The cover.** He stuffed the costume into an empty crypt niche, then hurried to the study with his medical bag and declared that Silas had been frightened to death.

**Where the will is.** In the false bottom of Quill's medical bag. He plans to burn it in the chapel's Hallows' brazier at midnight.

Every other suspect is hiding a secret that has nothing to do with the murder. Those secrets are the red herrings, and each one pays off with a clue that points back toward the truth.

## Six house rules

These rules keep the clock visible, the party moving and the finale short, so the session lands on three hours. Explain rules 1–5 at the start; save rule 6 for the accusation.

1. **Twelve Candles.** At 9:00 PM in-game, set 12 tealights or candle cards on the table. Every scene (searching a room, questioning a suspect, casting a ritual) snuffs one candle, which is 15 in-game minutes. When the last candle goes out, the midnight bell tolls and Act 3 begins, solved or not. If real time reaches 1:55, a sudden gust snuffs whatever candles remain.
2. **Split Up!** The party may split into groups. While split, one scene for every group costs only one candle in total. Give each group about 5 real minutes, then cut away on a cliffhanger. It doubles the investigation speed and rewards the oldest mistake in horror.
3. **Clues Always Land.** Core clues (marked ★) are never locked behind a roll. A failed check still finds the clue, but it costs an extra candle or triggers a jump scare. Rolls decide speed and bonus details, never whether the case can be solved.
4. **Treat Bag.** Each player starts with 2 pieces of wrapped candy. Eat one to reroll a d20, to add 1d4 to a roll after seeing it, or to ask the DM one yes-or-no question about the case. Hand out more candy for great roleplay, sharp deductions and excellent screams.
5. **Scaredy-Cat Bonus.** When a spooky moment calls for a Wisdom save, a player may fail on purpose and play it for laughs: shriek, dive into a suit of armor, leap into an ally's arms. They earn a candy. Outside combat, frightened from a scare ends at the end of the character's next turn.
6. **Unmask Him.** At the accusation, the party must name **who** did it, **how** and **why**. Each correct answer grants one Unmasking Edge in the showdown (see Act 3). Skeletons die to any hit, and the fight ends when Quill drops to 30 hit points or fewer: he tears off his mask, surrenders, and his minions collapse.

## Act 1: The masque (25 minutes)

Act 1 has one job: show the players the study while Silas is alive, introduce all six suspects, then kill the lights. Keep each scene to about 6 minutes.

### Scene 1: Arrival at dusk (6:30 PM)

> Fog rolls off the moor and pools around the iron gates of Ravenmoor Manor. Jack-o'-lanterns line the long drive, each carved with a raven in flight, their candlelight flickering orange through the mist. A stooped man in a patched coat raises a lantern as you approach. "Guests, is it? Mind the pond," he grunts. "She's restless tonight."

Bramwell Hatch, the groundskeeper, leads them in. Mrs. Crane, the housekeeper, takes their coats and asks them to wait upon Lord Silas in his study.

### Scene 2: The study (7:00 PM)

> Lord Silas Ravenmoor is a thin old man wrapped in a velvet robe, leaning on a raven-headed cane. Behind him, a fire crackles beneath tall bookshelves. On his desk sits a small iron lockbox and a vase of white lilies.

Silas opens the lockbox to show a scroll sealed in black wax: his new will. "Master Fennick, my solicitor, arrives at midnight to read it. If I'm dead before the bell, the old will stands, and that I will not allow." He won't say what the new will contains, only that "it will disappoint everyone at dinner." Asked about the legend, he chuckles: "Elspeth walks tonight, they say. She'll have to wait her turn."

Let the players poke around. What they see now matters later:

- The window is latched from inside, and thick ivy grows undisturbed across the outer sill.
- The lilies are already on the desk. Mrs. Crane put them there.
- The lockbox key hangs on Silas's watch chain.
- One bookcase stands slightly away from the wall (passive Perception 15 notices a cold draft).

### Scene 3: The Hallows' feast (7:30 PM)

> The Great Hall glows with a hundred candles. Paper bats hang from the rafters, and a roast boar steams on a table crowded with gourds and apples. Masked guests turn to look at you as you enter. Above, a dark minstrels' gallery overlooks the hall.

Introduce each suspect in one line, in costume (see The suspects): Lady Morwenna as a black swan, Edric as a headless horseman, Pip as a witch in an enormous hat, Dr. Quill as a plague doctor, Bramwell in his scarecrow coat, Mrs. Crane in housekeeper's black.

Plant three moments during the meal:

- At 7:45, Quill takes a glass of tonic from Mrs. Crane's tray, turns his back to add "his drops" and hands it to Silas. Characters with passive Perception 16 or higher notice the turned back.
- Edric and Silas argue loudly over money. Edric storms off to sulk.
- At 8:25, Silas excuses himself to rest before midnight. Quill leaves at 8:30 "to fetch medicines from my room." Over the next few minutes, Morwenna, Pip and Bramwell drift away too.

**When the party plays bodyguard.** They were hired to protect Silas, so expect it:

- **Guarding the door:** Silas refuses company in his study but lets them post a guard outside. That only deepens the locked-room puzzle, because no one passed the door.
- **Following Quill at 8:30:** he heads for the guest wing, waits in the dark, then slips down the servants' stair. Let the follower lose him without a roll, but reward the effort: they know he never lit a candle in his room.
- **Detecting poison at dinner:** let a sharp player catch it. Banshee's Breath shrugs off ordinary cures, and Silas insists on resting alone in his study anyway, so he still dies at 8:35. The party simply starts Act 2 already suspecting the tonic.

### Scene 4: The Lantern Widow (8:55 PM)

> A cold wind sweeps through the hall. One by one, the candles gutter. High on the minstrels' gallery, a green light blooms. A veiled woman in a pale gown stands there, a green lantern raised in one hand, glowing faintly from within. She lifts a single finger toward the family portraits. Then a gust howls across the hall and every candle dies at once.

In the dark, a scream rings out from the servants' stair (Mrs. Crane). Have everyone make a DC 13 Wisdom save against fright, and remind players of the Scaredy-Cat Bonus. By the time anyone reaches the gallery, it is empty.

### Scene 5: The body (9:00 PM)

The study door is locked from inside. When the party breaks it down (or Bramwell does with his shovel):

> Silas sits rigid in his chair, eyes wide, mouth open in a silent scream. Frost glitters on his fingertips. The window hangs open to the night, and wet footprints trail across the rug from the sill to the desk. The lockbox stands open and empty, and its key is gone from Silas's watch chain. White lilies lie scattered across the floor.

Dr. Quill pushes through moments later, medical bag in hand. After a quick examination he announces: "His heart gave out. He was frightened to death. The Widow has claimed her Ravenmoor." Morwenna, shaking, points out the obvious: the storm has flooded the road, no one can leave, and Fennick arrives at midnight. Without the new will, the old one stands.

Hand the players the suspect list (Handout 2), light the twelve candles, and start Act 2.

## The suspects

Five suspects look guilty and are hiding something harmless; the sixth, Dr. Quill, looks like the most helpful person in the house. Each innocent suspect gives up a ★ clue once their secret is out or respected.

### Lady Morwenna Ravenmoor

*Silas's second wife, 40s. Costume: a black swan.*

- **Looks guilty because:** she was cut from the new will, was cold to Silas all evening, and her hem is caked with mud.
- **Secret:** she plans to leave Silas at dawn. Her packed trunk sits in a carriage in the stables, and a love letter signed "R." is tucked in her glove.
- **Claimed alibi:** "In my room with a headache." Truth: loading the carriage in the stables.
- **★ Reveals** (Persuasion DC 14, or if the party finds the letter and promises discretion): from the stable yard at about 8:45, she saw a green glow moving past the chapel windows, ten minutes before the ghost appeared in the hall.
- **Play her:** clipped and haughty, fanning herself; she softens if treated with respect.

### Edric Ravenmoor

*Eldest son, 30s. Costume: a headless horseman, carrying his papier-mâché head under one arm.*

- **Looks guilty because:** he inherits everything under the old will, argued with Silas at dinner, and has been drinking steadily.
- **Secret:** ruinous gambling debts. In his coat is a letter (Handout 3) saying his debts have been bought by a new creditor who expects payment the day he inherits. ★ It is sealed in green wax stamped with a mortar and pestle.
- **Claimed alibi:** alone in the wine cellar, drinking. This is true.
- **★ Reveals:** at about 8:40 he heard footsteps and dripping water behind the cellar's old bricked-up arch, which backs onto the crypt. He has no idea who bought his debts, and goes pale when he learns that Quill's signet ring bears a mortar and pestle.
- **Play him:** bluster, self-pity and bad jokes about losing his head.

### Penelope "Pip" Ravenmoor

*Granddaughter, 19, a student illusionist. Costume: a witch with an enormous hat.*

- **Looks guilty because:** servants gossip that the Widow has been seen all month, and Pip's spell notes in her room include sketches of a veiled lady with a lantern.
- **Secret:** for a month she has used *minor illusion* to stage Widow sightings, hoping to get her grandfather to finally talk about Elspeth, the grandmother she never knew. She is terrified, because tonight's ghost was not hers.
- **Claimed alibi:** changing out of an ink-stained costume in her room. This is true.
- **★ Reveals:** "My illusions are just light. They don't put out candles, and they don't leave smudges. That ghost was solid, and its lantern had green glass in it." With a *detect magic* or an Arcana check (DC 12), she confirms there is no lingering illusion on the gallery.
- **Play her:** talks too fast, guilty and eager; she makes a good ally. If the party befriends her, she fights beside them in the showdown (see her actor sheet).

### Dr. Thaddeus Quill (the killer)

*Family physician and amateur alchemist, 50s. Costume: a plague doctor, with a beaked mask stuffed with herbs and tall buckled boots.*

- **His story:** "Silas's heart failed from fright." He volunteers to help, steering the party first toward the ghost, then toward Edric's debts, then toward Morwenna.
- **His lie:** "I went to my room to fetch my medicines." But Mrs. Crane found his room dark and empty at 8:45, and characters who watched the feast may recall his bag never left his side.
- **Tells:** he smells of frost-mint, the same smell as the poison. Glowing foxfire paste is caught under his fingernails (Perception DC 15 on a handshake or a close look). His buckled boots are wet with pond mud. His signet ring bears a mortar and pestle.
- **★ Carries:** the stolen new will, hidden in a false bottom of his medical bag (Investigation DC 15, or found automatically if he is searched after the accusation).
- **Under pressure:** he leans on his medical authority, then blames Edric. Inside a *zone of truth* he goes quiet and speaks only in careful half-truths. A man who refuses to answer is a clue.
- **Play him:** warm, grandfatherly and always "just trying to help." A little too helpful.

### Bramwell Hatch

*Groundskeeper and gravedigger, 60s. Costume: none, just his scarecrow-patched coat.*

- **Looks guilty because:** muddy boots, the smell of pond water, a shovel always in hand, and he mutters about the Widow constantly.
- **Secret:** every Hallows' Eve he lights warding lanterns around the pond to keep Elspeth at rest. He was there from 8:30 to 9:00. Decades ago he helped brick up part of an old passage under the house.
- **★ Reveals** (Insight or Persuasion DC 13, or if the party helps relight his lanterns or treats the legend with respect): a hidden passage runs from the chapel crypt up to the master's study, behind the bookshelves. He also mentions that two of his scarecrows have gone missing from the pumpkin patch.
- **Play him:** gruff and slow, tells the legend with relish and lowers his voice whenever the pond is mentioned.

### Mrs. Agatha Crane

*Housekeeper, 70s, in housekeeper's black, keys at her belt.*

- **Looks guilty because:** she was devoted to Elspeth and never forgave Silas for her death, and the scattered white lilies, Elspeth's flower, look like a ghost's calling card.
- **Secret:** she places white lilies in the study every Hallows' Eve in Elspeth's memory. The party saw them there at 7:00.
- **Alibi:** serving the feast, then on the servants' stair at 8:57 when the ghost brushed past her.
- **★ Reveals:** the "ghost" wore men's buckled boots and smelled of mint. When she turned down the beds at 8:45, Dr. Quill's room was dark and empty. She prepares the tonic tray, but the doctor always adds his own drops. The tonic glass is still unwashed in the scullery.
- **Play her:** stern and precise; she softens when anyone speaks kindly of Elspeth.

## The manor

The study, the gallery, the servants' stair and the chapel crypt hold the case; every other room holds a red herring or a supporting clue. Checks in brackets only speed things up (house rule 3). The matching printable card for each clue (C1–C14, H3–H5) is in the *Handouts & Clues* journal.

![Ravenmoor Manor, DM key: ground floor](modules/ravenmoor-manor/assets/maps/ravenmoor-ground-dm-key.webp)

The killer's route runs from the crypt, up the hidden stair inside the kitchen wall, to the study bookcase; everything Quill did tonight starts and ends in the crypt. The upper-floor and crypt DM keys are in the same folder.

### Study (the crime scene)

- ★ **Poison, not fright:** frost on the fingertips and blue-tinged lips in a warm room point to a cold-acting poison (Medicine DC 12).
- ★ **Staged window:** the wet footprints run from the sill to the desk but never back, and the ivy outside is undisturbed. The window was opened from inside (Investigation DC 12).
- ★ **The bookcase:** a pale scuff arc on the floorboards and a cold draft. Pulling *Hallowed Rites of the Ravenmoor Line* swings the case open (Investigation DC 14, or passive Perception 15).
- **Missing key:** the lockbox key is gone from Silas's watch chain. Whoever opened the box took it, and Quill plants it in Edric's coat at candle 8.
- Red herring: the scattered lilies (Mrs. Crane's).

### Secret passage

- A narrow, damp stair spiraling down to the chapel crypt.
- ★ Fresh candle wax on the steps, green glowing smudges on the walls, and a dropped sprig of frost-mint.

### Minstrels' gallery

- ★ Glowing green smears on the railing. Nature DC 12 identifies foxfire, a glowing fungus paste, not magic.
- *Detect magic* shows no magic at all: no illusion, no necromancy. Whatever snuffed the candles was not a spell.

### Servants' stair

- ★ A muddy print of a buckled boot on one step, and drips of pond water leading down toward the chapel corridor.

### Chapel and crypt

- ★ **The costume:** in an empty wall niche behind Elspeth's tomb, a bundle holding a pale gown, a veil, gloves crusted with foxfire paste, a green-glass lantern and a flask of pond water (Investigation DC 13).
- ★ **Moonsilver:** a silver-blue glint in a crack in the crypt floor (Arcana or Nature DC 14). Someone has been chipping at it.
- Two pumpkin-headed scarecrows stand oddly in the corner, and a chalk circle is half-hidden under a tarp. Quill has prepared for trouble; this foreshadows the finale.
- The Hallows' brazier stands ready to be lit at midnight.

### Scullery

- ★ Silas's unwashed tonic glass. The residue is frosted and smells of mint. Medicine or alchemist's supplies DC 13 identifies Banshee's Breath.

### Dr. Quill's guest room

- The bed is still unturned, and the fireplace has been cold all night.
- ★ An alchemy notebook (Handout 4) with a recipe for Banshee's Breath (Investigation DC 13).
- A stick of green sealing wax that matches the seal on Edric's debt letter.

### Library

- Old floor plans that show the passage from crypt to study (History or Investigation DC 13).
- ★ Silas's journal (Handout 5): his last entry notes that Quill keeps asking about the crypt.
- A portrait of Elspeth holding a lantern, its glass painted green.

### Wine cellar

- Edric's empty bottles and the old bricked-up arch. With an ear to the bricks, you can hear water dripping in the crypt beyond.

### Stables

- Red herring: Morwenna's carriage, packed trunk and a love letter from "R."

### Pond and pumpkin patch

- Bramwell's warding lanterns and Elspeth's memorial stone.
- ★ Two empty scarecrow posts, their straw scattered toward the chapel.

## Act 2: The investigation (80 minutes)

The party solves the case by reaching four conclusions, and each one has at least three clues leading to it, so missing any single clue never stalls the game.

| Conclusion | Clues that lead there |
|---|---|
| It was poison, not fright | Frost on the body; the tonic glass in the scullery; Quill's notebook; *speak with dead* |
| The ghost was a person | Foxfire smears on the gallery; Mrs. Crane's buckled boots; Pip's testimony; the costume in the crypt |
| The killer used the passage | The study bookcase; Bramwell's story; the library floor plans; Morwenna's green glow in the chapel; Edric's dripping behind the cellar arch |
| The killer is Quill | His empty room at 8:45; the mint smell; the mortar-and-pestle seal on Edric's letter; the foxfire under his nails; Silas's journal; the will in his bag |

### Running each candle

Ask the party where they go and who they talk to, run the scene, then snuff a candle. Answer questions generously. When players chase a red herring, let it pay off: every innocent suspect's secret leads to a ★ clue that points somewhere else. If the table goes quiet, have Dr. Quill wander in to "help" and drop a confident wrong theory. Players love proving him wrong.

**If the party cracks it early.** If they find the will in Quill's bag before midnight, he smashes a smoke flask, snatches it back and bolts for the crypt. Skip the accusation, grant the Who Unmasking Edge plus any How or Why they can state on the spot, and spend the saved time on the epilogue.

### Spells the party may use

Rulings for other spells and abilities, including *detect poison and disease*, *locate object* and *augury*, are in the *DM Guide* journal.

- *Speak with dead* on Silas (five questions, answered in a frightened whisper):
  - Who killed you? "I saw no one. Only the cold. My tonic tasted of mint."
  - Was it the ghost? "Elspeth would never harm me."
  - Where is the will? "Gone from the box. I heard the bookcase groan before the dark."
  - Who knew about the passage? "Hatch. Crane. And the doctor. I showed him myself when my legs failed."
  - Anything else? "Under the chapel... silver. Let it build something good."

- *Detect magic:* reveals no magic at all anywhere the ghost appeared. The chalk circle in the crypt glows with necromancy.
- *Zone of truth* or *detect thoughts:* the innocents give up their secrets. Quill dodges, goes silent, or blurts something about the crypt.

### Escalations

- **Candle 4: The Widow strikes again.** A crash in the library. Pip is found dazed on the floor, her spell notes gone, the air smelling of mint. She says the veiled ghost shoved her and whispered, "Stop meddling." If the party checks, Quill was "resting in his room" once again, and his room is still dark. Quill is silencing the one witness who can prove the ghost was not magic. Run this only while Quill is out of the party's sight; if he has stuck by them all night, he slipped away during the last scene change, so ask who was watching him.
- **Candle 8: A convenient culprit.** Quill calls everyone to the Great Hall. The lockbox key has turned up in Edric's coat, and Quill gravely accuses him. Edric is drunk and furious. The party can see that Quill handled the coat last (Insight DC 13 senses he is performing). Planted evidence that conveniently solves everything is its own clue.
- **Candle 11: The bell tower creaks.** Lights from Fennick's carriage appear on the drive. Quill excuses himself "to say a prayer for Silas in the chapel." He is off to light the brazier.

### If the party is stuck

Climb this ladder, one rung per stalled candle:

1. A suspect remembers something: Mrs. Crane mentions the boots, or Bramwell mentions his missing scarecrows.
2. A player eats a candy to ask a yes-or-no question (house rule 4).
3. A spooky nudge: the study bookcase creaks open by itself in a cold draft, or green light flickers in the chapel window.
4. Pip pieces it together aloud: "Grandfather took his tonic from the doctor every night..."

## Act 3: Unmasking (50 minutes)

At midnight the party names who, how and why in the Great Hall; whatever they say, Quill bolts for the chapel crypt and the session ends in one fight there.

### The accusation (15 minutes)

> The bell in the tower tolls twelve. The doors swing open and a damp, nervous halfling in a dripping cloak hurries in, clutching a satchel. "Fennick Barrowdown, solicitor. I'm here to read Lord Ravenmoor's will. Which will, I'm told, is... in question?"

Gather every suspect and let the party make their case. Ask them to name **who**, **how** and **why** (house rule 6), then award Unmasking Edges:

| They name correctly | Unmasking Edge |
|---|---|
| **Who:** Dr. Quill | No surprise for Quill. Every character rolls initiative with advantage. |
| **How:** poisoned tonic, plus the passage or the costume | Pip hands out sprigs of counter-herb. Every character has advantage on saves against Banshee's Breath. |
| **Why:** Edric's debts, the moonsilver or the stolen will | Edric and Bramwell rush in behind the party and hold the coffin lids shut. The round 1 lair action does nothing. |

**If they accuse Quill,** he laughs softly, smashes a smoke flask on the flagstones and flees for the chapel crypt. **If they accuse someone else,** Quill nods gravely, "Then justice is done," and slips away. Mrs. Crane or Pip notices a minute later and raises the alarm. Either way, the party catches him in the crypt with the will in one hand, inches from the lit brazier.

### The showdown (35 minutes)

> Green fire roars in the brazier. Dr. Quill stands inside a chalk circle, his beaked mask gleaming, the will held over the flames. "Ravenmoor's dead belong to me tonight." Coffin lids grind open. In the corner, two scarecrows lift their carved pumpkin heads, candlelight blazing behind their grins.

**The crypt:** about 50 by 40 feet, dim light. Stone coffins give half cover. Two iron candelabras can be knocked over (an action) to set a 10-foot square ablaze for 2d6 fire damage to anything inside. The scarecrows hate that. The brazier sits at the far end. The fire is not hot enough to take the will until the round 3 flare. From then on, if Quill starts his turn next to it holding the will, he can drop it in as a bonus action. A character next to Quill can snatch the will with an action and a contested Athletics or Sleight of Hand check.

**Enemies:**

| Party size | Dr. Quill | Pumpkin-headed scarecrows | Skeletons |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 characters | 162 Hit Points | 1 | 4 |
| 6 characters | 162 Hit Points | 2 | 4 |

Under the 2024 rules this is a Moderate encounter: 4,400 XP for six characters (4,450 once the two lair skeletons join), and 3,700 for five (3,750 with them).

If the clock runs past 2:35 in real time, Quill surrenders at 50 hit points instead of 30. Run all the skeletons on one initiative count to keep rounds quick.

**Lair actions** (initiative count 20, as the midnight bell keeps tolling):

1. **Round 1, The dead rise:** two more skeletons burst from coffins.
2. **Round 2, Candles gutter:** a ghostly wail. *Wisdom Saving Throw:* DC 13, each player character. *Failure:* the Frightened condition until the end of its next turn. The Scaredy-Cat Bonus applies.
3. **Round 3, Green flame:** the brazier flares. *Dexterity Saving Throw:* DC 13, each creature within 10 feet of it. *Failure:* 7 (2d6) Fire damage.

### The unmasking

When Quill drops to 30 hit points or fewer, the skeletons collapse into dust, the scarecrows slump, and he tears off his beaked mask.

> "Thirty years I bandaged this wretched family's wounds, and I'd have owned every stone of this house by morning. You lot were supposed to stay scared!"

## Endings and epilogue (15 minutes)

The ending turns on two things: whether the party caught Quill, and whether the new will survived the brazier. Fennick pays the party's fee from the estate, and how much depends on the outcome.

| Outcome | What happens | Reward per character |
|---|---|---|
| Quill caught, will saved | Fennick reads the new will in the Great Hall. The manor becomes the Hollow Brook orphanage, the moonsilver pays for it, and every family member and servant gets a modest stipend. Quill waits in the cellar for the dawn constable. | 200 gp, plus the lantern below |
| Quill caught, will burned | The old will stands and Edric inherits. Shaken and sober, he swears before witnesses to honor his father's wishes. Whether he keeps that promise is a hook for another night. | 150 gp |
| Quill escapes | He vanishes through the pond fog with the will's ashes and a grudge. The party has made a recurring villain. | 100 gp |

The new will holds one last line for the party: "To whoever unmasks my killer, should it come to that: my cane, and my thanks." The raven-headed cane is a fine walking stick worth 50 gp.

### The real ghost

End every version of the night with this:

> As you step out into the grey before dawn, Bramwell's warding lanterns around the pond flicker, one by one, from orange to green. On the still water stands a pale woman in a veil, holding a lantern of green glass. She bows her head to you, once. Then the fog closes, and she is gone.

On Elspeth's memorial stone, the party finds her old green-glass lantern, dry and warm. It works as a *lantern of revealing*. Was the Widow real? Let the players argue about it on the way home.
