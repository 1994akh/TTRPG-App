# Ashford Roads decisions: answers (10 Nov 2026)

From the [Ashford Roads Decisions](https://claude.ai/artifact/9rAyu4vdVagLaLEd8re3bD) page. 57 of 59 took the recommendation; two were answered with notes (below). The system and the module are built on these.

## Answered with a note

| Id | Question | Your note |
|---|---|---|
| tone-1 | What "Dungeonpunk" means in Wayfinder | Brume is the power system: binding (class and grade), charge (refined fuel), spells and signatures all come from it. Use the lore in Enkrid's Journey. |
| tone-2 | What "Gaslit fantasy" means in Wayfinder | Sky ships are rare because they are new (about 30 years old) and take a long time to build. The brume engine is what makes them fly. |

## Recommendations taken

| Id | Question | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| tone-3 | How dark is Ashford? | Grim streets, hopeful crew. The cruelty is systemic (the Compact, the Bench, how monsterkin and fae are treated) and the danger is real, but the story is about found family, wonder and getting out. Deaths happen off-screen or to strangers in Act 1. |
| tone-4 | How strange is the Gloam? | Uncanny, not gory: it moves wrong, sounds are muffled near it, the lamps don't light it, and it is never seen clearly until the sun holds it. |
| tone-5 | Weather and light in Ashford | Fog rolls in off the brume every night and burns off by mid-morning everywhere except Refinery Row, which stays clear and warm. Night streets are pools of amber charge-light in grey. |
| tone-6 | Humour | Keep the banter: dry, quick, character-led, never undercutting a serious beat. |
| world-1 | What brume is | A grey fog of unfixed Possibility lying on the open sea. Breathed in deep water it slowly unwrites things that aren't bound; refined, it becomes charge; bound into a person, it becomes their class and grade. |
| world-2 | What charge is and what it powers | Refined brume sealed in glass cells. It lights lamps, runs lifts, pumps and engines, and drives Artifice spells. Charge cells are graded like everything else, and a full F-grade cell costs about a day's wage. |
| world-3 | Sky ships | They exist but almost nobody has seen one: the legends' expedition was rumoured to use the inventor's brume engine to fly. In Act 1 they are a dream and a rumour. |
| world-4 | The Compact | A chartered trading company that became a government in all but name: refinery, licences, insurance, wardens, debt. Polite, legal and merciless. In Act 1 it is everywhere but never the villain on screen. |
| world-5 | The Bench | The courts and record-keepers of the Reach: charts, deeds, inheritances and contracts. Bench families are old money that holds the maps, and a chart isn't true until the Bench stamps it. |
| world-6 | The Wrights | A faith of makers who teach that the world was wrought, and that fixing things is holy. Chapels keep the clocks and the schools. Tinkers are often Wright-raised. |
| world-7 | Law on the streets | Two forces: the city Watch (underpaid, answers to the Bench) and Compact Wardens (well paid, answer to the refinery). The surety bond for monsterkin is Compact policy the Watch enforces. |
| world-8 | Kelder's Wall | The edge of the charted Reach: a standing bank of brume that never lifts, graded far above anything in the Reach. Ships that go past it rarely come back. |
| world-9 | Why insurers matter | The Compact underwrites every voyage out of Ashford. No policy, no berth, no harbour will take you. That's the real reason class is a gate. |
| world-10 | How fae are treated | Distrusted, not hated: people think fae leak Possibility and bring bad luck aboard. Polite avoidance, higher prices, captains who 'have no berths'. Binding hits fae harder (Enkrid collapses). |
| world-11 | How monsterkin are treated | Legal second-class: surety bonds, one inn, lower wages, written policy. Plenty of ordinary people are kind one-to-one; the system isn't. |
| world-12 | Who lives in Ashford | Mostly Founding peoples (humans, dwarves, half-orcs), a big beastkin quarter by the fish market, few fae, and monsterkin only on the quay. |
| world-13 | Coin names | Keep cp/sp/gp/pp in the rules and on sheets, with Reach names in the text: copper bits, silver marks, gold crowns, platinum charters. |
| story-1 | Protagonist 2 and 3's names | Keep Rook and Tova as working names until you choose. |
| story-2 | The other drafted names | Keep them as placeholders; rename any in the note and the module follows. |
| story-3 | What Petra saw from her window | Grey fog moving against the wind out of the street grates at night, and a light under the street where no one should be. A hook for what the refinery dumps below. |
| story-4 | What the Gloam is | Brume run-off from the refinery that pooled in the dark for forty years until it started wanting. Sunlight fixes it and it falls apart. Nobody in Ashford wants that known, which quietly sets up the Compact. |
| story-5 | Did the broker know? | She suspected from the sellswords' silence and Petra's story, and sent the cheapest crew she could find. Not evil, a broker. |
| story-6 | How Hollis Tarrant pays | He pays in full and in public, so Refinery Row sees he's generous, and asks them never to mention where Petra was found. |
| story-7 | The three dead sellswords | Give them names in the journal (Draft) and a Coldwater crew mark, so Coldwater can come back later. Their journal is a handout. |
| story-8 | The pocket watch's engraving | You write it. Until then the flashback and the item show a blank engraving line you fill in once on the item. |
| story-9 | How the flashback plays at the table | Read-aloud with a sepia journal page, played as narration with one or two choices for young Enkrid (no dice), then back to the steps. |
| story-10 | Who your character is in Act 1 | Same as the JRPG: you make one custom character, the trio are fixed characters you also run, and your character's intro depends on their race family. |
| story-11 | Your character's reason to need a ship | Leave it to you at character creation: a short 'Why I need a ship' line on the sheet that the journal prompts refer back to. |
| story-12 | What binding feels and looks like | The brume goes in through the hands and chest; everything goes white and too-sharp for a moment; it leaves a faint grey vein mark (the 'binding mark') that darkens with each grade. |
| story-13 | The binding fee | 50 gp per F-grade binding, x3 per grade after (150 gp at E), with the numbers above. |
| story-14 | How much Rourke shows in Act 1 | Charming and reasonable on the quay; one detail that sticks (his crew is armed like mercenaries, not sailors) that pays off on island 3. |
| story-15 | How Act 1 ends at the table | The Tern leaves; a last read-aloud; then one line where a scruffy captain on the next berth has been watching them. Title card. My Story asks you to write the end of Act 1. |
| story-16 | How many days Act 1 covers | Four days: arrive and take the job (day 1), the Undercourses (day 2, the Gloam at noon), paid and bound (day 3), the missed ship (day 4 morning). |
| story-17 | Side work in Act 1 | Include the broker's board (a few F-grade jobs as a roll table) and the outer Undercourses rat cull as optional, off by default in the Director. |
| story-18 | The legends' journal page 1 | Hide it in Act 1 at the Lantern and Lee (Dunmore kept a page Captain Varr left with a bar tab). You write its text; the module has a blank handout. |
| play-1 | Who's at the table | Solo: you run the world as GM and play all four heroes. The Act 1 Director shows the next beat, posts read-aloud and opens the right map, so you rarely need the journals mid-scene. |
| play-2 | An oracle for solo play | Add a small oracle macro: ask a question, pick likely/even/unlikely, get Yes / Yes, and / Yes, but / No, but / No / No, and. |
| play-3 | Read-aloud | Post read-aloud lines to chat as the speaker (with their portrait), one tap per block, so the chat log becomes the story so far. |
| play-4 | Dice | Foundry dice by default, with an 'enter my own roll' option on every check. |
| play-5 | How My Story works | Keep that: a prompt per beat (what your character said, what changed, a line to remember), the Director's 'Write this beat' button, and an automatic session log of what happened. |
| play-6 | Where your story goes afterwards | Add a 'Copy my story' button that turns My Story into clean text you can paste into Word. |
| play-7 | Maps or theatre of the mind | Battle maps for the two fights and the Scene; the rest as scene art you can use or ignore. |
| play-8 | Zones on a grid | Track zones on the sheets; the grid is only for placing tokens so the picture reads. |
| play-9 | How hard should Act 1 be | Fair but tense: Old Gnaw can drop one hero if you're careless; nobody dies in Act 1 unless you turn on the Undertaking setting. |
| rules-1 | Raise Guard | 1 action: +2 AC until the start of your next turn. |
| rules-2 | Aid | 1 action: an ally within Near gets +2 on their next check this round. |
| rules-3 | Stabilise | Automatic for an Engaged ally at 0 HP; they stop Dying and stay at 0 HP. |
| rules-4 | Recovery checks while Dying | At the start of each turn: d20 + CON save vs DC 12. Success = stable; three failures = dead (or Defeated with the Undertaking setting off). |
| rules-5 | Possibility points pool | 3 + Combat EL, refilled on a long rest. |
| rules-6 | Profession signatures before binding | Usable before binding, from the same pool (3 points when unclassed). |
| rules-7 | Scenes: when a Setback hits | On a critical failure always, on a failure when the approach says so, and once at the end of every round the clock moves. |
| rules-8 | Short rests | A short rest (one watch) spends hit dice: roll each, add CON mod. A long rest restores everything. |
| rules-9 | Unarmed and improvised weapons | Unarmed d4; improvised d4 with −2 to hit unless your profession uses the tool (a Tinker with a pry bar uses it at d6). |
| rules-10 | XP in Act 1 | Track profession XP from Scenes, and give a milestone at the end of Act 1; class XP starts after binding. |
| look-1 | Visual touchstones | Victorian dockside and refinery, wet stone undercity, brass instruments, ledgers and wax seals. Think Dishonored's streets and Arcane's undercity, kept your own. |
| look-2 | Portraits | Keep the silhouettes until you have art; every portrait is one image you can swap on the actor. |
| look-3 | Music | An empty playlist per location (Ashford, the Exchange, the Undercourses, the Gloam, the Binding House, the inn, the quay) for your own tracks. |
